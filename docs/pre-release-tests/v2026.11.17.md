# v2026.11.17 pre-release tests

Tested on 2026-11-17, the day it came out, on the lab whose Controller
and nodes ran v2026.11.13 - the newest release
([v2026.11.13's report](v2026.11.13.md)). What the pre-release
changes:

- **recycling keeps a member's address** (#643, #647): the newcomer
  takes the member's static address over, the nodes announce the
  addresses they add (gratuitous ARP), a member can be moved to another
  address (**Address**), and a configuration keepalived or BIRD refuses
  fails the apply (#648);
- **Let's Encrypt managed by the cluster** (#644, #645): a tab on the
  cluster, the configuration in the cluster's version without its
  secrets, locked on the members, **Test**, **Renew**, an account key
  import; an apply skips what a member already has;
- **a Controller starting cold while GitHub is very slow** knows the
  releases sooner (#642, #646), and a node's **Update** page shows an
  upload's progress (#642);
- janusd may stat `/sbin`'s entries (#641);
- the static builds on Alpine 3.24.4, AWS-LC 5.14.0, golang.org/x/crypto
  0.59.0 (#639, #656).

The campaign: the release measured first (health, boot stages,
throughput from the CI runner and the build box, the pages, the
router's ARP entry when a node replaces another at the same address),
the Controller's update through its updater and the image factory, then
what the pre-release brings - two throwaway machines on static
addresses in a cluster with a unicast VRRP virtual IP, the member
holding the VIP recycled into a newcomer taking its address, then moved
to another address and back, with the VIP probed and the lab router's
ARP entries and both members' VRRP states sampled every 0.2-0.3 s; the
refusals; a configuration keepalived refuses; a cluster of two Let's
Encrypt machines against the lab's private ACME CA (through the proxy,
then ordered by the Controller); the Controller restarted without its
release cache with GitHub 4 s away; an upload followed on a node's
Update page; then the core: the cluster updated in place under load, a
VRRP and a BGP failover, HAProxy reloads, firmware reboots and kexec on
both kernel tracks, a rollback and back, HAProxy's cost per request
with the release and the pre-release alternated on node 01 by
rollbacks, the pages and the nodes' health.

## Verdict

**What the pre-release changes works on the lab. The member holding
the virtual IP was recycled into a newcomer that took its static
address in 70 s without a lost probe (0 of 1,450, every 0.2 s), the VIP
moving to the other member and back, never two VRRP masters; the lab's
router took the newcomer's MAC for the address within 0.1 s of the
address being added - the gratuitous ARP of #647 (the Proxmox proof saw
43.8 s before it). The machine's record, the members' addresses and both
members' `keepalived.conf` followed. **Address** moved the member away
and back without a lost probe; a `/32` is not announced. A version
keepalived refuses failed at once with keepalived's message, nothing
touched. The cluster's Let's Encrypt works through the proxy and when
ordered by the Controller: one Test answer per member, Renew, the
member that orders changed with the mode kept and the follower
answering the challenge, an imported account key on every member, the
lock and the drift, no HAProxy reload for a Let's Encrypt change, and
no secret in a version or the journal. The cold-started Controller knew
the release and the pre-release within 51 s and the 🔒 badge within
66 s, GitHub 4 s away; the Update page shows the file, the bytes and the
bar. The core campaign is as before: the cluster updated in place in
35.6 s with no probe lost during it, the same failovers, boot times and
cost per request, zero AVC denials, CIS 33/33. My opinion: ready to
promote** - findings 1 to 4 are worth an issue each, none blocks; the
decision is yours.

## What the pre-release brings

### Recycling with the address kept (#643, #647)

Two throwaway machines from the pre-release's factory image of the
lab's schematic (VRRP, BGP, nftables, node_exporter, the QEMU agent),
one on each KVM host, each with a single interface on a static address
of the management network; a cluster of the two: a stick table over
the peers, keepalived from a template - unicast, `unicast_src_ip {{
.Member.Address }}`, `unicast_peer` from `.Peers`, a VIP of their own on
that network, priorities 150/100 -, and a firewall matching the
interface. Machines ready in 10 and 13 s, the cluster made in 2.3 s,
its VRRP version applied in 11.9 s, each member's keepalived naming the
other.

Measured from the build box during each move: an HTTP probe of the VIP
every 0.2 s, the lab router's ARP entries for the VIP, the members' and
the temporary addresses every 0.2 s (a script on the router itself,
lines stamped as they arrived), each member's VRRP state through its
node page every 0.3 s, and each member's keepalived log afterwards.

**The member holding the VIP recycled, "It takes its address"**
(`keep_address`, `temporary_address` in the API):

| From the call | Step (cluster journal) |
|---|---|
| +0.2 s | a new machine like it, on the same host |
| +8.7 s | the newcomer joins **drained** on the temporary address; both members' keepalived rendered again for three members |
| +14.4 s | the cluster's version applied on the newcomer |
| +15.5 s | **the member drained** - the other member takes the VIP -, then leaves; the ticket key ring renewed |
| +28.4 s | "nothing answers at" its address: its machine destroyed |
| +29.4 s | the newcomer's keepalived stopped, its interface given the member's address on trial |
| +64.1 s | the move confirmed at that address (finding 1: 34.6 s of this wait) |
| +64.7 / +70.0 s | the version applied again on the other member, then on the newcomer: every member knows the newcomer at that address; the old node forgotten |
| **+70.1 s** | the newcomer back in service, the call done (69.6 s) |

- **The VIP: 0 of 1,450 probes lost**, served by the old member until
  +15.1 s, by the other one until +70.6 s, by the newcomer from +70.8 s.
- **Never two masters**: the old member BACKUP and the other MASTER at
  +15.5/+15.6 s; the newcomer BACKUP on its temporary address, stopped
  while it moved, BACKUP at its new address, MASTER at +71.5 s as the
  other went BACKUP - the keepalived logs give the same seconds.
- **The router's ARP entry for the address taken over switched to the
  newcomer's MAC 0.1 s after the address was added on it**, and its
  entry for the VIP at the moments the master changed.
- Afterwards: the machine's record on the static address with the
  newcomer's MAC, the node registered there, the old one gone from the
  node list and from its host, `Member.Address` the address, and both
  `keepalived.conf` rendered with each other's addresses.

**Address** (`POST .../members/{name}/address`), the newcomer - holding
the VIP - moved to another free address and back:

| The call to the cluster idle | 45.2 s | 47.2 s |
| Drained / keepalived stopped / confirmed | +3.7 / +4.8 / +39.5 s | +3.5 / +4.4 / +39.4 s |
| VIP probes lost | **0 of 632** | **0 of 583** |
| The router's ARP entry for the new address | the newcomer's MAC 0.2 s after the address was added | the same |

The VRRP states read through the node page lag behind keepalived by up
to 1.6 s: polled every 0.3 s they showed both members MASTER for up to
one second at the handover back, which the keepalived logs, the VIP's
probes (served by one member, then the other, none lost) and the

**Refused** (each on a throwaway cluster or a version of this one):

**Address** on a member whose address came from DHCP is not refused:
it moved it to a static address in 40 s, the cluster told and the
machine's record turned static - a way to fix a member's address, not
a refusal the brief expected.

**A configuration keepalived refuses** (#648): a version whose
`keepalived.conf` names an interface the nodes lack failed in 0.2 s,
checked on the first member: "keepalived.conf: (keepalived.conf: Line
12) WARNING - interface nosuch0 for vrrp_instance VI_KA doesn't exist;
Non-existent interface specified in configuration"; both members kept

### Addresses announced (#647)

- **An address added by a network change**: a `/24` and a `/32` added
  to a node's interface on trial, the router holding a stale entry
  with another MAC for each: the `/24`'s entry took the node's MAC
  0.1 s after the call; **the `/32` was not announced**, its entry
  keeping the other MAC for the 30 s watched. The node's configuration
  put back after.
- **At boot**: a machine destroyed and another made at its address on
  the other host, the router's entry and a ping every 0.2 s - on
  v2026.11.13 the entry took the new MAC 8.9 s after the creation, by
  the router's own resolution (the new node answering), on the
  pre-release 6.9 s, the entry updated in place by the announcement.
  With traffic flowing, as here, the router resolves the address again
  quickly either way; the takeover above is the case that needed the
  announcement.

## Findings

1. **A move waits 34 s for nothing** - minor, the move itself is right.
   When a member's address moves (a takeover or **Address**), the
   Controller's `NetworkConfigApply` stream was opened to the old
   address, which disappears without a reset: the Controller waits for
   its 30 s timeout, then tries the confirmation at the old address
   first (4 s), then at the new one, which answers - three times out of
   three, 34.6, 34.7 and 35 s between the address added and the
   confirmation. No traffic is lost (the member is drained, its
   keepalived out), but it is half of a takeover's 70 s and of the
   move's trial window. Ending the wait once the node said "applying",
   or trying the new addresses first, would take it away.
2. **Two refusals come after a machine is made** - minor. A takeover
   in a cluster of one member, and one under a firewall naming
   `.Member.Address`, are refused only after a new machine was created
   (then destroyed), about 10 s later; the notes say "refused before
   anything is touched". The members were never touched; the refusals
   that need no machine (DHCP, no temporary address) are reported as an
   error after a 202, not as the request's own answer.
3. **The first HTTP-01 order of a cluster fails when the challenge
   reaches a follower** - minor. Setting the cluster's Let's Encrypt
   hands the configuration to the member that orders first; it orders
   at once (here within the same second), while the others still answer
   HTTP-01 with their own account - they take the orderer's when the
   version reaches them, about a second later. The CA validated on the
   follower and the order failed (403); Renew fixed it. Behind a VIP or
   a balancer sending challenges anywhere, the first certificate fails
   until the next attempt. Arming the followers before the orderer
   starts would avoid it.
4. **Renew from the cluster doesn't hurry an order the Controller
   holds back** - minor. In "Ordered by this Controller", after a
   failure (here the Controller's ordering policy), **Renew** on the
   cluster wakes the Controller's loop, but the certificate keeps its
   back-off (5 to 10 minutes); "Order what's due now" on the
   Controller's Let's Encrypt tab clears it. The notes say Renew goes
   through the Controller "at once" when it orders.
5. **Two isolated probe losses after the in-place update** - not the
   release's: one probe of the VIP and one of the anycast address lost
   at the same instant, and one more of the VIP 3 s later, 1.5 minutes
   after the update ended; nothing on either node at that moment, and
   the build box, which ran the probes, was busy with another workload.
   The update itself lost nothing.

The VRRP state the node page shows lagging keepalived by up to 1.6 s
(above) is worth knowing, not a finding of this release.
