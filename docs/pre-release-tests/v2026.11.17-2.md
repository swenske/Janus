# v2026.11.17-2 pre-release tests

Tested on 2026-11-17, the afternoon it came out, on the lab where
v2026.11.17 - the newest release, promoted that morning - was measured
that same morning ([v2026.11.17's report](v2026.11.17.md)). What the
pre-release brings, the fixes of that campaign's findings:

- **a member's move confirmed at once at its new address** (#663, and
  #666 for janusctl's message): a takeover or **Address** no longer
  waits about 34 s on the address it leaves;
- **a recycle or Address that can't keep the address refused at once**
  (#663): a 409 with the reason, before anything is made;
- **a cluster's first HTTP-01 order no longer races the others** (#662):
  every member answers with the ordering member's account before it
  orders; and the cluster's **Renew** orders at once when the Controller
  orders;
- **what an apply takes on or lets go is in the cluster's journal**
  (#664).

Every change is the Controller's, janusctl's or the cluster engine's
they share: `cmd/janusd`, the root filesystem and the kernels are the
same as v2026.11.17's (no node change between the two tags - only
`internal/clusters` and `internal/netconfig`'s endpoint helpers, which
the Controller and janusctl use). So this was a **short, targeted
campaign**: no throughput A/B and no full boot series - the nodes run
the same bytes. The lab's two nodes and the Controller's longterm and
Let's Encrypt machines stayed on v2026.11.17; only the Controller was
updated. Each finding of v2026.11.17's campaign was replayed the same
way as there, on new throwaway machines (v2026.11.17), and compared
with what that campaign measured.

## Verdict

**Each of v2026.11.17's four findings is fixed on the lab. The move of
a member to its new address is confirmed 5.1 to 5.7 s after its
keepalived stops (34-35 s that morning, three times out of three): a
takeover of the member holding the virtual IP takes 41.9 s instead of
69.6 s, an **Address** 16-18 s instead of 45-47 s, still without a lost
probe of the VIP (0 of 636, 361 and 362, every 0.2 s), never two VRRP
masters, the lab's router taking the newcomer's MAC within 0.1-0.3 s.
Seven requests that can't keep an address are each a 409 in 6 to 19 ms
with the reason, no machine and no libvirt domain created, the cluster
never busy (a 202 then an error before, a machine made and destroyed
for two of them). A cluster's first HTTP-01 order, validated on the
member that doesn't order - the exact case that failed in the morning -
succeeds without Renew: the follower answers with the orderer's
account from the instant it's told, no invalid authorization at the
CA. With "Ordered by this Controller", a certificate the name policy
refused is ordered 13 s after the cluster's **Renew** once the rule is
added (5 minutes' back-off kept before). An apply leaving `acme` out of
`managed` says so in the journal, and taking it back too. The
Controller's pages are 0 problems in both themes with the same buttons
as v2026.11.17's; every node 0 AVC, CIS 33/33. My opinion: ready to
promote** - the decision is yours.

- **The first build didn't publish** (below): #663 had changed
  janusctl's message for a trial that cuts the caller off - reproduced
  here 2 runs out of 2, fixed by #666 (2 out of 2 here, CI green), so
  the published janusctl is right.
- Two test runs on CI failed once and passed when run again
  (`qemu-console-flood-test`, `qemu-cluster-upgrade-bgp-test`); neither
  touches what changed.

## A member's move (#663)

Two throwaway machines of the lab's schematic (keepalived among its
extensions), one on each host, each with a single interface on a free
static address of the management network; a cluster of them with a
replicated stick table, keepalived from a **unicast** template on
`{{ .Member.Address }}` and `.Peers` (a VIP of their own, priorities
150/100) and a firewall matching the interface - the same cluster as
the morning's. During each move, from the build box: the VIP probed
every 0.2 s, the lab router's ARP entries every 0.2 s, each member's
VRRP state through its node page every 0.3 s, each member's keepalived
log afterwards.

| Move | Call to idle | keepalived stopped → "moved … confirmed there" | VIP probes lost | Two masters | The router's ARP entry for the address |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Takeover of the master ("It takes its address", a temporary address) | **41.9 s** (morning: 69.6 s) | **5.1 s** (morning: 34.7 s) | 0 of 636 | never | the newcomer's MAC 0.1 s after the address was added |
| **Address**, to another free address | **16.4 s** (morning: 45.2 s) | **5.7 s** (morning: 34.7 s) | 0 of 361 | never | the new address on the member's MAC 0.2 s after |
| **Address**, back | **18.2 s** (morning: 47.2 s) | **5.6 s** (morning: ~34 s) | 0 of 362 | never | 0.3 s after |

The takeover's journal, from the call: the newcomer's machine +0.2 s,
the replacement announced +8.2 s, the newcomer joined drained on its
temporary address +8.9 s, the version on it +14.5 s, the old member
drained +15.7 s and gone +16.0 s, its address free +28.7 s, the
newcomer's keepalived stopped +29.7 s, **confirmed at the address
+34.8 s**, the version applied again on both, the old member forgotten
and its machine destroyed, the newcomer back in service +41.9 s. The
VIP was served by the old master until +15.1 s, by the other member
until +42.4 s, by the newcomer from +42.6 s.

Never two masters: in each move, keepalived's logs put one member's
"Entering MASTER" and the other's "Entering BACKUP" in the same second,
and the VIP's probes pass from one to the other without a loss; the
0.8-1.8 s of "two masters" the node pages' polling shows is their state
lagging keepalived's, as that morning.

A member on DHCP moved to a static address with **Address** (a
conversion, accepted as before): 12.2 s (40 s that morning), confirmed
5.7 s after it was drained.

## A cluster's first HTTP-01 order (#662)

The exact case that failed that morning: two new machines with the Let's
Encrypt extension, **never configured for Let's Encrypt**; a cluster of
them with HTTP-01's rule on port 80; the lab's private ACME CA (Pebble)
reachable from the lab only through the Controller's host, its DNS
answering every name with **the member that doesn't order**, so each
HTTP-01 challenge reaches the follower; Let's Encrypt set on the cluster
through the proxy, **no account key imported**, a certificate over
HTTP-01 and a wildcard over DNS-01. The account each member answers
challenges with was read every 0.1 s on the follower (a made-up token
on its port 80).

- Before: each member answered with its own account.
- The setting (202, done in 2.4 s), its journal in order: "the other
  members answer HTTP-01 with the account … orders with, before it
  orders with the new configuration: its first challenges may reach any
  of them" (new); 13 ms later "Let's Encrypt's configuration given to …,
  which orders, with its secrets (…names…) - the others pull them from
  it"; the version made, "manages Let's Encrypt's configuration from
  version 3 on", applied on both.
- **The follower answered with the orderer's account from that first
  entry's instant** (within the 0.1 s sampling), the CA's validations
  began in the same second: **the HTTP-01 certificate issued 10 s after
  the setting, the wildcard 18 s after; no invalid authorization, no
  403 in the CA's log, no Renew** (that morning: 403, the certificate
  failed, Renew needed). At the next check the follower pulled both;
  both members serve the same serials.

Going from "Ordered by this Controller" back to the proxy later wrote
the same entry first; both members moved from the Controller's account
to the orderer's, nothing ordered again.

## Renew with "Ordered by this Controller" (#662)

The Controller's account and DNS provider set, the lab CA's directory
allowed, **a name policy allowing only another domain**; the cluster
set to "Ordered by this Controller" (both members on the Controller's
account). The cluster's **Renew**: refused by the Controller - "the
Controller's ordering policy doesn't allow the name … for this node" -
next attempt in 5 minutes. The rule for the lab's names added, then the
cluster's **Renew** alone: **the HTTP-01 certificate issued 13 s later,
the wildcard (DNS-01, its propagation wait) 21 s later**, the errors
cleared, nothing waiting - without "Order what's due now" (that morning:
needed). The follower pulled both at the next check.

## The managed domains in the journal (#664)

A version (a TLS frontend on the certificates) applied with
`managed: ["haproxy", "files"]` - Let's Encrypt left out: the journal
says "the cluster stops managing Let's Encrypt's configuration from
version 4 on: the lock released, each member keeps what it runs and is

version applied with `acme` again: "the cluster manages Let's Encrypt's
configuration from version 4 on: the same on every member, locked
there". Both members serve the certificate on the frontend throughout.
