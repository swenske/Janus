# v2026.10.30 pre-release tests

Tested on the morning of 2026-10-30. It is
[v2026.10.29-6](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/releases/tag/v2026.10.29-6),
the release, with:

- [#430](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/430): BIRD starts with its
  `haproxy_*` protocols disabled while HAProxy is gated, and the cluster
  update tests join the image build;
- [#429](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/429): a node on a
  pre-release that changes its image stays on that pre-release;
- [#426](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/426): a log target paused
  and resumed;
- [#428](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/428): the Controller's
  audit names what each entry is about, and comes a page at a time with
  filters;
- [#427](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/427): the CPU model of a
  Proxmox hypervisor's machines;
- [#410](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/410): qemu-guest-agent
  11.1.3.

## Verdict

**Ready to promote.** On the lab's real nodes:

- **The baseline holds**: failovers, firmware and kexec reboots, the
  rollback and HAProxy's throughput are at the level of the release,
  measured the same morning. Zero AVC denials and the CIS audit 33/33
  on every node.
- **BIRD stays quiet while HAProxy is gated**: on the release,
  re-applying `bird.conf` with the gate closed sent the router an update
  and a withdraw; on the pre-release, nothing.
- **The cluster updated in place**, under load, in 35.5 s: anycast 0
  lost, the VIP one probe. **A drained member rebooted** sent no route
  until undrained.
- **A machine on the pre-release changes its image and stays on it**,
  with the guest agent 11.1.3 (#410) added, then removed.
- **A paused log target** gets nothing, then catches up from the LOGS
  partition. **The audit page** names what each entry is about,
  removed things included.

One defect, in the release process rather than the release: rerunning
the failed publish job replaced the published assets with other bytes
([finding 1](#findings), [#433](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/issues/433)).
The release as it stands now is what was tested.

## What this pre-release changes

### BIRD held while HAProxy is gated (#430)

On node 02, HAProxy is stopped through the API, so the gate closes and
BIRD's `haproxy_*` protocols are held down. The node's own `bird.conf`
is applied again while held, then HAProxy is started. Both versions are
measured with the node's own exporter: the BGP updates and withdraws it
sent to the router.

| Moment | v2026.10.29-6 (updates / withdraws sent) | v2026.10.30 |
|---|---|---|
| Before | 3 / 2 | 1 / 0 |
| HAProxy stopped (held) | 3 / 3: the route withdrawn | 1 / 1: the route withdrawn |
| 10 s after the apply, still held | **4 / 4: an update, then a withdraw** | **1 / 1: nothing sent** |
| HAProxy started | 5 / 4 | 2 / 1 |

On the release, the apply re-announced the anycast route for a moment
while HAProxy was down. On the pre-release, nothing leaves the node
until HAProxy is back. The router's routes, polled about every 0.45 s
throughout, never showed node 02 while it was held, on either version:
the release's leak lasted less than a poll.

### Updating a cluster in place

The cluster of the lab's two nodes was made on the release: a stick
table replicated between them, and VRRP from a template. It was then
updated in place to the pre-release under load (`wrk` on the VIP from
the build box, probes every 50 ms from a runner):

- **35.5 s** from the call to the end. Node 02 (the backup) went first:
  drained, updated, rebooted on slot B, back and announcing 18 s later.
  Then node 01, the master: drained at 08:50:25, back at 08:50:37,
  announcing at 08:50:39.
- **VIP: 2 lost out of 5,758.** One (0.05 s) as the VIP moved to node 02
  when node 01 drained ([finding 3](#findings)), one 2 minutes later
  (path noise). **Anycast: 0 out of 5,747.**
- `wrk`: 13,753 req/s, p99 5.86 ms, 16 read errors.
- The journal lists every step. The stick table kept its 3 keys and
  their counters. Both members came back drained, as the release keeps
  a drain across a reboot, and were undrained by the update itself.

### A drained member across a reboot

On the pre-release, node 02 was drained, rebooted through the firmware
and undrained once back, under load. Its exporter's BGP counters were
sampled every second:

- drained: one withdraw sent, and the router dropped its route;
- back **still drained**, with its `haproxy_*` protocols held down;
- **0 updates sent** from its boot until the undrain (184 samples). The
  first update left 0.8 s after the undrain, and the router listed node
  02 again 6 s after it;
- VIP 0 lost out of 4,293, anycast 0 out of 4,284; `wrk` 14,039 req/s,
  p99 5.29 ms.

That reboot took 186 s, against 10 s otherwise. The node's own boot
stages (kernel 1.66 s, init 1.77 s, API 2.16 s) put the time in the
firmware ([finding 2](#findings)).

### A pre-release machine changing its image (#405, #410)

The lab's Controller (updated to the pre-release, 4.91 s without an
answer) made a machine from the pre-release's image, with the default
schematic: ready in 11 s. Its node page's calls (Update › Apps) then
added qemu-guest-agent:

- the image factory's catalog was the pre-release's (7 extensions). The
  update check still said "no update": the pre-release isn't offered as
  one;
- **the bundle prepared was the pre-release's**, not the release's:
  `target_prerelease` true, ready after 39 s;
- installed, the machine was back **8.0 s** later on v2026.10.30 (slot
  B) with qemu-guest-agent **11.1.3** running. The host read the
  guest's four interfaces and its version through the agent.

Removing it the same way gave the pre-release's default schematic: ready
at once (the release's own bundle), back after 8.6 s on v2026.10.30
(slot A), no extension.

The first try at removing it was refused by the node:
`rootfs.squashfs sha256 1936ac99… doesn't match requested f27fe792…`.
The Controller had cached the release's sums before the rerun replaced
the assets ([finding 1](#findings)). The node checking the sum is what
it should do. The second try came after the Controller's 10-minute
cache.

### Pausing a log target (#424)

The lab's nodes send their logs to the log bench (Elasticsearch)
through the Controller's profile "lab":

- `janusctl system log-forwarding pause elastic` on node 01 refused:
  "set by the Controller (… profile lab): pause or resume the target on
  its profile". It names the profile.
- Paused on the profile (the Controller's API, as its page saves): node
  01 and the machine said `disabled` **2.1 s** later, the target's API
  key kept.
- A janusd line was made while paused, by an audited call. Nothing
  reached Elasticsearch during the pause.
- Resumed, both nodes said `ok` again within 2.1 to 3.2 s. **The
  machine's line arrived, read back from its LOGS partition.**

Node 01 has no LOGS partition: it was installed before LOGS existed. As
the guide says, its janusd line made during the pause never came,
since memory isn't kept for a paused target. Its audit entry and its
event for that call did come after the resume, from STATE.

### The audit page (#425)

On the lab's Controller in headless Chromium, light and dark: no
console error, no failed request, axe clean.

- The newest 100 of 2,484 entries. **Load older** gave 200, the total
  unchanged.
- The filter by the campaign's machine, from the page's address: 17
  entries, each naming "Node lab-janus-62" with a link to its page.
  The node's refused update shows there too.
- After the campaign's machine was removed (through the Controller,
  gone in 7 s), the same filter still gave its 17 entries, each
  "lab-janus-62 (removed)". The removal itself names "Machine
  lab-janus-62", and the cluster's deletion "Cluster lab", both marked
  removed.

The newest 100 entries named a subject only 3 times, and that was
right: 91 came from one of the campaign's scripts, which ran with its
session expired and posted to `/nodes//api/...` with an empty node.
Those were refused (401), recorded, and name no node. Another 4 were the
Controller's log forwarding settings, which are about no single thing.

## Findings

1. **Rerunning a failed publish job overwrote a published release**
   ([#433](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/issues/433)). The rerun
   rebuilt the bundle and replaced all 44 assets about 7 minutes after
   the release came out. The bytes differ: `rootfs.squashfs` was
   `f27fe792…`, and is now `1936ac99…`. It also pushed
   `swenske/janus-controller:v2026.10.30` again with another digest:
   the lab's Controller, updated from the first push, runs a digest the
   tag no longer names. A Controller that cached the first sums offered
   them for 10 minutes, and nodes refused the bundle, as they should.
   Two things to fix: a rerun should only add what is missing, and
   never change a published asset or version tag. Two builds of one
   commit should also give the same bytes. Nothing in the release is
   wrong now: it was tested as it stands.
2. **A firmware reboot of 186 s** (node 02, the drained member's
   reboot): the node's own boot stages took 2.16 s, so the time was the
   firmware's. The lab's machines keep their UEFI variables in a file,
   and OVMF compacts that store every few dozen boots: that is the slow
   reboot root-caused on 2026-10-25
   ([#307](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/307)). Not the
   pre-release.
3. **One VIP probe lost as the master drained** during the cluster's
   update in place (0.05 s, one probe of 5,758). VRRP moved the VIP to
   the backup as the master's gate closed, and one request in flight
   didn't make it. v2026.10.29's update in place lost none, and the
   drained member's reboot this time lost none either: one probe is at
   the level of the path's noise. To keep an eye on.
4. **A node's refusal shows as 502 on the Controller.** The refused
   update above (a sum that doesn't match) reached the page, and the
   audit, as `502 Bad Gateway: Upgrade stream: rpc error: code =
   FailedPrecondition …`. The relay gives any error of the Upgrade
   stream 502, while the node answered, and with a clear reason. Minor:
   the message is right, and only the status is a gateway's.
5. The lab's profile has a second target, a Logstash outside the lab's
   reach: `failing` throughout, as in the campaigns before. It's how
   the lab is set up, not the pre-release.

## Not tested

- **The Proxmox CPU model (#427)**: the lab has no Proxmox. #427's own
  tests cover the setting, its drift and Terraform. Its finding stands:
  `x86-64-v2-AES` and `x86-64-v3` lack PCLMULQDQ, so HAProxy's TLS 1.3
  still picks ChaCha20 on them. Only `host` or a named CPU model gives
  AES-GCM.

## What the lab keeps

The lab's Controller and nodes 01 and 02 on v2026.10.30, on their own
again. The cluster was deleted in 1.1 s, and each node's `haproxy.cfg`
was put back as it was before the cluster: its other files had stayed
identical. The longterm and Let's Encrypt machines stay on v2026.10.28-2.
The campaign's machine was removed, and the runners' load kit taken
off.
