### The build's own changes

- **What the kernel cache and the incremental images save**: the first
  build since [#218](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/218) without
  a kernel change. Every test job but one was shorter than v2026.10.22's,
  where every runner rebuilt both kernels: the longest job 12 min 11 s
  against 15 min 48 s, eight of the eleven jobs a quarter to two thirds
  shorter. Across the jobs' logs, 100 steps found the root filesystem
  "up to date" and 66 took the disk image "from the pristine copy".
- **A real Keycloak signs in to the Controller** in the Controller's job
  (`make controller-sso-test`): every scenario passed - roles from
  groups, refusals, janusctl's sign-in page kept across the round trip,
  signing out of Keycloak, linking, a group and the rules changed,
  Keycloak's second factor, a provider disabled and removed.
- **The publishing job ran out of disk** on one runner: Docker's build
  cache had grown to 27 GB, over the 20 GB the runner's daemon keeps,
  and the Controller's release images (one tag per release) are never
  removed by the job's tidy step - only its per-commit ones. Pruned by
  hand while the runner was idle (19 GB free again), the job rerun
  alone. The tidy step should prune both.

### The minute-long update reboot, again

In the rolling update, node 01's reboot took 57 s from its Upgrade call
to its kernel starting, where it takes 8 s - the same 57 s as node
02's in v2026.10.22's campaign; from its kernel on, the boot was as
usual (the API 2.0 s after the kernel started). The console shows
nothing in between but the usual lines: HAProxy's proxies stopping, the
kernel's restart, the firmware loading the boot entry.

Right after, the same rolling update under the same load, to
v2026.10.22 (the other slot) and back to v2026.10.22-2, with both
nodes' serial consoles timestamped as they were written: **four update
reboots, all 16.2 s** from drained to back in service - the Upgrade
call, HAProxy stopped 4.7 s later, the restart at once, the firmware
2.4 s, the kernel, the API 2.5 s after it. Not reproduced.

What it isn't, as far as the evidence goes:
- **janusd's own wait**: before a reboot it stops HAProxy within 12 s
  at most (2 s draining, 5 s for its soft stop, 5 s after SIGTERM), and
  its shutdown steps only write files;
- **the traffic HAProxy had served**: node 01's had served 10.3 million
  backend connections before its slow reboot, node 02's in
  v2026.10.22's campaign had just been restarted;
- **the console's content**: the slow reboot's raw console is the same
  as a fast one's, line for line, apart from HAProxy's counters and the
  slot's name.

The same fixed delay twice suggests a timeout somewhere between the
restart and the firmware handing over to Linux. Next: janusd timing each
step of its reboot path in its log, and the consoles timestamped through
every campaign's updates, until it happens again under watch.

### Single sign-on on the lab's Controller

A Keycloak 26.8.1 (the image CI's test pins) started on the lab
Controller's own host, its certificate from a CA made for the test -
the CA given to the Controller -, with the realm of CI's test: alice in
`/janus-admins`, bob in `/janus-readers`, carol in no group. The
provider set up through the Controller's API, then headless Chromium
signing in through Keycloak's own pages:

- **Test**: the discovery document's issuer matches the settings, one
  signing key, the client authenticated with `client_secret_basic`, one
  warning (Keycloak doesn't list the mapper's `groups` claim);
- **bob** (`/janus-readers`): a reader, signed in;
- **alice** (`/janus-admins`): an admin, asked to set a second factor
  up first - the lab Controller's policy asks every admin for one;
- **carol** (no group): refused, the page showing the reason and a
  reference;
- **signing out** (bob): through Keycloak's end-session endpoint, with
  the ID token as a hint and the Controller as the address to come back
  to; Keycloak then asked for bob's password again;
- **Keycloak's second factor**, the provider asked for `acr` `1`: alice
  signed in with nothing more asked;
- **what the Controller keeps**: `sso.json` on its data volume owned by
  the Controller's user (65532), mode 0600, the client secret not in it
  in the clear; its log names the ID token's claims - not their values -
  once;
- one browser console line: Chromium's own for carol's refused finish
  (403), as for a wrong password's 401.

Then the provider, the two accounts it had made and Keycloak removed:
the lab's Controller is as before.
