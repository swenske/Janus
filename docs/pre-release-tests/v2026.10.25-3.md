# v2026.10.25-3 pre-release tests

Tested in the night of 2026-10-25. It is
[v2026.10.25-2](v2026.10.25-2.md) with:

- **a cluster started from a node**: new machines, the first taking the
  node's state node to node before the cluster is made from it
  ([#315](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/315));
- **Let's Encrypt in a cluster**: the secrets' source orders, the others
  serve its certificates, answer its HTTP-01 challenges and pull its
  renewals ([#316](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/316));
- **a RouterOS router in front**, its ECMP routes kept by the cluster
  ([#318](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/318));
- **the Controller's libvirt machines without a UEFI variable store**
  ([#312](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/312));
- docs: a balancer's retries, the cluster page's screenshot, the
  previous campaign's report (#311, #313, #314).

The same kernel and HAProxy as v2026.10.25-2, on the same lab: two KVM
hosts (16 GB with a RAID cache, 32 GB with plain SATA drives), the lab's
RouterOS 7.24.5 router. The baseline is a light one, compared with
v2026.10.25-2's figures; then what this pre-release changes.

## Verdict

**Not ready as is.** Two of its four changes don't hold in the lab; both
have a fix, neither in v2026.10.25-4, cut meanwhile.

- **The machines kept their variable store** (#312): on hosts prepared
  as [hypervisors](../hypervisors.md) says, the Controller's account may
  not read the host's firmware list, and the machines silently got
  libvirt's choice, with a store - [finding 1](#findings),
  [#326](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/326). Done the way #326
  does it, a machine ran 45 firmware reboots in 4.7 to 5.7 s, while one
  with a store took 41.1 s once.
- **Let's Encrypt changing hands** (#316) re-ordered every certificate,
  and the old orderer answered HTTP-01 with the wrong account until the
  next check - a renewal failed (403) -
  [finding 2](#findings), [#325](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/325).
  The rest works as described: one member ordered, its challenge
  answered by the other, which refused a renewal, pulled the certificate
  and each renewal at the check, and served them.
- **A cluster from a node** (#315) works as described, rehearsed as the
  migration of a load balancer already serving: the node untouched, one
  grant and one export by the transfer certificate in its log, the
  machines with its configuration, files and certificates under their
  own identity, in 1 min 46 s. A `bind` on the node's own address
  doesn't stop the machines - the guide said it would
  ([finding 3](#findings)).
- **RouterOS** (#318) works as described: ECMP routes made at once, a
  member's route removed before its drain, a route changed by hand put
  back at the check, the router's own route untouched, check-gateway
  leaving out a member forced off. What ECMP does to open connections
  and how RouterOS spreads flows wants saying
  ([finding 4](#findings), [#324](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/324)).
- **The baseline holds**: the rolling update under load lost **nothing**
  on the VIP nor on the anycast route; zero AVC denials, the CIS audit
  33/33.

## What this pre-release changes

### A cluster from a node (#315)

Rehearsed as the migration of a load balancer already serving: node 01,
the lab's VRRP master, given a map, an error page and an uploaded
certificate first. Through the Controller's page in a browser: **New
cluster → New machines**, two, anti-affinity required, both hosts, 1
vCPU and 1 GiB, the letsencrypt, node_exporter and QEMU agent
extensions, **Start from** node 01.

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| The form | **Start from** lists the Controller's nodes ("its configuration, files and certificates"), the help says what goes over and what doesn't; no console error |
| Created | 202; the cluster made **1 min 46 s** later, both machines included: "seed44-1 took [node 01]'s state, node to node - 65 file(s), without its identity nor its network - and restarts into it", back 12 s later "serving [node 01]'s configuration", joined, version 1 taken from it, the other member pulling the certificate |
| A note in the journal | "the exported node ran another image (…): what an extension this image lacks was configured for stays unused" - node 01 has BGP and VRRP, the machines didn't |
| Node 01 | its `haproxy.cfg` identical (SHA-256), its files and certificate identical, in no cluster; its log and audit: `StateExportGrant` by the Controller's user `cluster` (os:admin) - "seed44-1 may pull this node's state once, until [10 minutes later] (identity false)" -, then `StateExport` by seed44-1's transfer certificate (`janus:state-transfer`), **from the machine's own address**, 66 files, 115 ms: nothing through the Controller |
| The members | both serve node 01's `haproxy.cfg` (the same SHA-256), the same three files, the certificate loaded - each under **its own CA**, hostname and address |
| Kernel tuning | not exercised: node 01 has none saved |

**A `bind` on the node's own address** - the case of a load balancer
binding the address it holds: node 01 given a frontend bound to its own
front address, then a one-machine cluster seeded from it. **Made in
22 s, no refusal**: the member listens on node 01's address - Janus sets
`net.ipv4.ip_nonlocal_bind` by default. The guide said such a bind makes
the machines' HAProxy refuse to start ([finding 3](#findings)). For a
migration it's the simple path: once the old node is gone, its address
can become the cluster's virtual IP and the members serve it as
configured. Node 01's configuration was put back (identical), the
cluster removed.

### Let's Encrypt in a cluster (#316)

The cluster above, its secrets from seed44-1. A private ACME CA
(Pebble, Let's Encrypt's test CA) reached through the lab's proxy, its
DNS answering every name with **seed44-2's** address: it validates
HTTP-01 on the member that doesn't order. Two certificates: `site`
(HTTP-01) and `wild` (DNS-01).

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| A version with the HTTP-01 rule | applied in 1 s; each member answers a challenge with its own account |
| Let's Encrypt configured on seed44-1 (its page, through the proxy) | `wild` valid; `site` failed - 403, seed44-2 answering with its own account: it is told who orders at the next check |
| **Check now** | journaled "the cluster's Let's Encrypt certificates are ordered by seed44-1: the others order nothing, serve them and answer HTTP-01 with its account", seed44-2 pulled the configuration and certificates; seed44-2: **Ordered by seed44-1**, its challenge answer seed44-1's account; `site` issued at once, validated on seed44-2 |
| A renewal asked of seed44-2 | refused: "seed44-1 orders this cluster's certificates: renew them there - this node pulls them" |
| A version with a TLS frontend on the certificates' directory | both members serve both certificates, verified against the CA's root |
| `wild` renewed on seed44-1, **Check now** | "seed44-2 pulled seed44-1's renewed certificate(s): wild" - served by seed44-2 at once |
| seed44-2 as it follows | both certificates shown **due**, no renewal time - [finding 2](#findings) |
| The secrets' source changed to seed44-2 on the page, **Check now** | **seed44-2 ordered both certificates again** 8 and 16 s later, though it held valid ones; seed44-1 "Ordered by seed44-2" but answering challenges **with its own account**; no journal line - [finding 2](#findings) |
| The CA pointed at seed44-1, a renewal on seed44-2 | **403**: seed44-1's wrong answer |
| The next check | journaled "ordered by seed44-2", seed44-1 pulled, its answer seed44-2's account; the same renewal went through |

## Findings

1. **The Controller's libvirt machines keep a variable store on a host
   prepared as the docs say.** The driver asks the host's domain
   capabilities for its firmware images; that needs `connect.write`,
   which the generated polkit rules don't grant - and shouldn't: it also
   lets the account suspend the host. The call failed and the machine
   got libvirt's choice, without a word. `controller-libvirt-test` runs
   on a host without polkit, so it passed.
   [#326](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/326) defines the machine
   as before, reads back libvirt's choice and makes it stateless before
   the first start - with the account's own rights, one path for every
   host; proven in the lab as that account.
2. **Let's Encrypt changing hands in a cluster** (three defects, one
   cause each): a pulled certificate keeps no record of its CA, so a
   follower shows it **due** and, the day it orders, **orders it
   again** - with Let's Encrypt, every change of the secrets' source
   re-issues every certificate (5 duplicates a week at most); and the
   check that sees the new source tells the others "it orders" **before
   knowing its account**, so for a check's time (10 minutes) they answer
   HTTP-01 with their own and the new orderer's challenges reaching
   them fail. [#325](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/325) records
   where a pulled certificate comes from, completes the handover in one
   check (the new source told alone, its account read, the others told
   with it), and tells the members one at a time with their stick
   tables learnt between - a member whose challenge answer changes
   reloads HAProxy. `qemu-cluster-acme-test` passes with it.
3. **The guide on a cluster from a node** named a version that doesn't
   exist (v2026.10.26 - it is v2026.10.25-3), and said a `bind` on the
   node's own address stops the machines: with Janus's defaults it
   doesn't ([#324](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/324)).
4. **RouterOS, what the guide should say**
   ([#324](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/324)): ECMP keeps no
   state, so a route removed or added moves open connections to another
   member, which resets them - the drain delay can't let them finish,
   new connections lose nothing; RouterOS's default hash keeps all of a
   source's connections on one member, and `l4` with FastTrack resets
   about one connection in five; check-gateway takes 10 to 20 s; the
   REST access open to the Controller only needs an input rule on a
   router that drops by default.
5. **The cluster page's screenshot** drifted again (the balancer card's
   words); [#321](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/321), merged
   since, took it again.

A note on the lab's tooling: the Controller's link to a node comes back
a few seconds after the node does (its reconnection waits up to 5 s), so
a call through the node's page right after a reboot is a 502 - the
first reboot loop lost every other call to it.
