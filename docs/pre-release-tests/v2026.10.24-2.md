## Verdict

**Not ready as is.** Nothing regressed: the Controller's update, the
image factory, the rolling update under load (nothing lost on the VIP),
the failovers, the reboots and the throughput are level with
v2026.10.24's. Recycling, the release's main change, did what it says
under load: the VRRP master recycled into a new machine in 19.5 s with
**no request lost** on the VIP, the VIP moving once, the stick table
kept to within the requests in flight, the old machine destroyed. The
ticket keys are renewed when a member leaves, proven by TLS resumption
on real nodes. Zero AVC denials. Two findings in the clusters, both
fixed and proven on real boots:

1. **Under traffic, the member serving it still reads "catching up"**
   - on the Clusters page, in `janusctl cluster status` and in
   `janus_cluster_peer_table_synced` -, "in sync" again once the traffic
   stops. #269 compared the right counters, at one instant: with
   thousands of updates a second the peer is never at the very last
   one. [#273](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/273).
2. **A node removed from its cluster stays drained, and nothing on it
   says so** - out of service for its VRRP and BGP, the lab's node 02
   no longer backing up the VIP -; **added to a cluster again, it
   joined drained**. [#274](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/274).

A next pre-release with #273 and #274.

### Recycling a member under load

The lab's Controller made two machines on the lab's hypervisor from
the pre-release (1 vCPU, 1 GiB, the lab's schematic; ready 12 s and
10 s after the call), each with one interface by DHCP - a recycle
clones a machine's interfaces, and a static address can't be cloned -,
then a cluster of them with a stick table, a TLS frontend, and VRRP
from a template: a VIP of their own, each member's priority a variable
(150 and 100). Then, `wrk` on the cluster's VIP from a runner (about
19,800 req/s for 4 minutes) and the probes from another: **Recycle** on
the master, as the page does (`POST /api/clusters/{id}/recycle`).

| | What happened |
|---|---|
| Time | 19.5 s from the call to done: the new machine ready 10 s after it, joined drained, its secrets and version, the stick table learnt, in service as the master was drained, the master out, its machine destroyed |
| Requests lost on the VIP | **0** of 4,610 probes; `wrk`: 4,746,893 requests, 8 read errors - its keep-alive connections cut once as the VIP moved |
| The VIP | moved **once**, from the old master to the newcomer (it went MASTER once; the other member never) |
| The stick table | on the newcomer, 4,751,498 = the 3 counted before + the 4,751,503 sent - the 8 `wrk` requests cut as the VIP moved: exact |
| The newcomer | named after the cluster, its priority the old master's, the same networks, a new MAC |
| The ticket keys | renewed on the remaining member and pulled by the newcomer; a TLS session from before the recycle no longer resumes |
| The journal | each step: the new machine, joined drained, the version, the drain, the old member out, the ring renewed, its machine destroyed |
| The page | the cluster's tabs in headless Chromium, both themes, axe: no console error, no failed request, nothing from axe; **Recycle** offers "a new machine like it" first for a machine member. The newcomer, serving, shown "catching up" (finding 1) |

The cluster then deleted - each machine making a ring of its own - and
both machines destroyed through the Controller.

## Findings

1. **Under traffic, the member serving it reads "catching up".** With
   `wrk` on the VIP, node 01's peer read `SYNCED 0/1` in `janusctl
   cluster status`, `janus_cluster_peer_table_synced 0`, and the
   Controller's check marked node 01's tables "catching up" - every
   sample, for as long as the load ran; idle, `1/1` within seconds. The
   rule from #269 - the peer's acknowledgement reached the table's last
   local update - is right, but read at one instant: at thousands of
   updates a second some are always in flight. Any busy cluster shows
   it, and the page's "in sync" header next to a member "catching up".
   [#273](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/273): when a peer is
   behind at a first read, janusd reads `show peers` again half a
   second later and counts it in sync if it acknowledged by then
   everything the first read showed - a peer really behind stays so.
   `qemu-cluster-test` now checks it under load (eight clients, 255,963
   requests in 25 s): the released janusd fails at the first sample
   (`0/1` for both peers), the fix passes.
2. **A node removed from its cluster stays drained, silently, and joins
   again drained.** A removal drains the member first and the drain
   stays - on purpose: the node may still run the cluster's VRRP or BGP
   configuration. But `janusctl cluster get` on node 02 showed only
   "Cluster: none" (the node reported its drain to members only), its
   VRRP status "HAProxy: not answering" with priority 1/100: the lab's
   VIP had lost its backup with nothing saying why. And nothing undrains
   a node that joins again - added back to a cluster it would have
   stayed out of service there. [#274](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/274):
   the node reports its drain, member or not; the journal says a
   removed member left drained and how to undrain it; adding a member
   or creating a cluster puts a drained node back in service (a
   recycle's newcomer still joins drained on purpose). Proven in
   `qemu-clusterctl-test`.
3. **A recycle needs interfaces by DHCP**: a member with a static
   address can't be cloned into a new machine (two machines would hold
   it). The lab's machines have static front addresses, so the
   recycled cluster had its own VIP on the management network. As
   designed and documented - a member with a static address is recycled
   into a node one names -, but worth knowing before building a cluster
   meant to be recycled.
4. **The recycle journals the old member's drain twice** (the swap's,
   then the removal's own drain, already done). Cosmetic.
