# v2026.10.31-2 pre-release tests

Tested on 2026-10-31, the morning the pre-release came out. It is
[v2026.10.31](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/releases/tag/v2026.10.31),
the release, with:

- [#451](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/451) (fixes
  [#134](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/issues/134)): the Controller's
  TLS identity keeps a CA of its own, which signs its certificate and
  renews it 35 days before its end;
- [#452](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/452) (fixes
  [#135](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/issues/135)): the fleet's
  issuing CA renewed with the recovery kit;
- [#450](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/450) (fixes
  [#446](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/issues/446)): a reboot or a
  power-off stops the extension services after HAProxy's soft stop -
  keepalived and BIRD last.

## Verdict

**Ready to promote**, in my opinion. On the lab's real nodes and its
Controller:

- **The baseline holds**: the Controller's update, the cluster updated
  in place under load with nothing lost, failovers, HAProxy's throughput
  at the release's level, zero AVC denials and the CIS audit 33/33 on
  every node. Each reboot and rollback takes about one second more,
  the time the extension services now take to stop (#450, below).
- **The Controller's identity (#451)**: the lab's Controller, made long
  before, kept serving its first certificate after the update and said
  until when; janusctl contexts, the relay's node and new machines
  followed by themselves. Forced to switch, it served a certificate its
  CA signs, and nothing that had been given the CA broke: janusctl
  without signing in again, the relay, every node page, a cluster apply,
  and a machine created afterwards that registered at once.
- **The issuing CA renewed with the kit (#452)**: every running node had
  the new bundle within the second, a node switched off throughout took
  it by itself 3 minutes after it came back, and only then did
  janusctl's certificates come from the new CA - which reaches every
  node, a node of v2026.10.21 included. The Controller kept it all
  across a restart.
- **A rebooting node ends its BGP sessions (#450)**: the lab router got
  a `Cease` (administrative shutdown) from each node before it went
  down; with the release, the session was only cut by BFD once the
  kernel restarted.

Three minor findings, none in the way of a promotion
([Findings](#findings)).

The release, v2026.10.31, was measured the same morning by its own
campaign ([its report](v2026.10.31.md)); this campaign measured it
again where a new scenario needed it (#450, below). The probes ran from
the build box, every 50 ms; the load from the build box or a runner.

| Step | v2026.10.31 | v2026.10.31-2 |
|---|---|---|
| The Controller's update by its updater | no answer for 4.80 s (47 of 606 probes) | 5.31 s (52 of 613), none slower than 0.5 s; installing it without confirming it's a pre-release refused (409) |
| The bundle for the lab's schematic | ready 100 s after the ask | ready 44 s after the ask |
| The cluster updated in place under load | 81.5 s; VIP 2 lost out of 5,453, anycast 0 | **33.7 s; VIP 0 lost out of 3,323, anycast 0 out of 3,321**; `wrk` 13,467 req/s, p99 6.18 ms |

## What this pre-release changes

### A rebooting node's BGP and VRRP (#450)

A node rebooted under load (`wrk` on the VIP, probes on the VIP and the
anycast address), while the lab router's view was read every 0.23 s:
each BGP session's state, the anycast route's next hops, the BFD
sessions and the MAC behind the VIP. The lab's sessions run BFD
(200 ms × 3) and a 9 s hold time. Times are from the reboot call:

| | v2026.10.31 | v2026.10.31-2 |
|---|---|---|
| Node 02 (VRRP backup): anycast route withdrawn | +2.19 s (HAProxy stopped, BIRD's gate) | +2.05 s (BIRD's log) |
| Node 02: the BGP session ends | +4.97 s, by **BFD**, after the kernel restarted; the router logs only `Idle` | +4.16 s, BIRD shutting down: the router logs **`Received Notification … cease: administrative shutdown`**; seen down at +4.37 s |
| Node 01 (VRRP master): the VIP moves to node 02 | +2.80 s | +2.87 s |
| Node 01: the BGP session ends | +4.90 s, by BFD | by a `Cease`, before the node's `rebooting now` line |
| Requests lost | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 on node 02's reboot; on node 01's, one on each address, 29 s and 60 s after the call, the node long back (the path) |

Both releases measured with and without the cluster: the same times.

- **The BGP session goes down at the reboot**, by a `Cease` sent while
  the node is still up, before its reboot line. With the release, BIRD
  died with the kernel and the router had to notice the silence; in this
  lab BFD notices within 0.6 s, so the gain is under a second. A router
  without BFD would have waited for its hold time - 9 s here, often 90 s
  or 180 s.
- **The VIP**: it already moved at HAProxy's stop with the release,
  because keepalived tracks HAProxy's health (priority 150 to 50 on the
  master, 100 to 1 on the backup); keepalived's own stop comes a second
  later, after the VIP has gone. A VRRP setup without that tracking is
  the one that gains here.
- The anycast route itself was withdrawn at HAProxy's stop with both
  releases (BIRD holds its `haproxy_*` protocols while HAProxy doesn't
  answer), so nothing was lost either way.

### The Controller's identity (#451)

**A Controller made before, after the update.** The lab's Controller
dates from 2026-10-01: a self-signed certificate valid until 2027-10-01,
pinned by janusctl contexts, given to its machines' NoCloud and to a
node using the Let's Encrypt relay. Before the update, a throwaway
machine was put in relay mode with that certificate as `proxy_ca`
(`test-path` to Let's Encrypt's staging directory through the relay:
OK), and two janusctl contexts signed in: one pinned by fingerprint, one
with `-controller-ca` given the certificate. After the update:

- the log at start: `identity: until 2027-08-27 the Controller serves
  its first certificate, then one its identity CA signs: …`;
- the certificate served on the page's, the registration and the relay's
  ports: **the same fingerprint as before**;
- `controller-info`'s `ca_cert_pem`: **two certificates**, the new
  "Janus Controller identity CA" (until 2046) and the first certificate;
  `identity_ca_fingerprint` beside;
- **Settings › HTTPS certificate**: the identity CA's fingerprint and
  end, the certificate's end, the switch date (8/27/2027), *Download the
  identity CA* - light and dark, no console error, axe clean;
- both janusctl contexts kept working without signing in again, and the
  release's janusctl too; after a sign-in, each context held
  `controller_identity_ca`;
- the relay's node: **60 s after the start**, `identity: node …: its
  Let's Encrypt relay settings now check the Controller with its identity
  CA` - its `proxy_ca` went from one certificate to two -, and
  `test-path` still went through.

**The switch, forced.** The Controller was given one more
`JANUS_CONTROLLER_ADVERTISE_ADDRESS` (a name, in its Compose file) and
restarted:

- `identity: the Controller's certificate renewed - valid until
  2027-10-31, signed by its identity CA`; the new certificate kept every
  name of the first plus the new one, and `openssl verify` with the
  identity CA accepts it; `controller-info` gives the CA alone;
- **janusctl**: both contexts worked without a new sign-in, and their
  certificates were renewed through the API token (forced, by setting
  their expiry in the past) against the new certificate. The release's
  janusctl, on the same context, refused it (`certificate signed by
  unknown authority`, with the new fingerprint) - as the upgrade notes
  say, contexts follow once janusctl is updated; the pre-release's
  janusctl then used that context again;
- the relay's node: `test-path` OK;
- every node page (seven nodes, light and dark): no failed request, axe
  clean, apart from one older node (finding 2);
- a cluster apply: 0.6 s, both members applied;
- **a machine created afterwards** registered 5 s after it started,
  ready in 13 s; its NoCloud's `controller_ca_cert` is the identity CA;
- the browser: a new certificate, so a browser that had accepted the
  first one warns once more (expected; the headless checks ignore
  certificate errors, so this wasn't looked at in a real browser).

The first advertised address also becomes the address the Controller
suggests to nodes (finding 1).

### The issuing CA renewed with the kit (#452)

Before: bundle version 1 on all seven nodes (`GET /api/fleet`'s
`bundle_version`, and `janusctl access trust`), the issuing CA until
2028-10-06. One node, a Controller machine outside the cluster, was shut
down. Then, in headless Chromium, the Fleet card's *Renew the issuing
CA…*, its confirmation, the kit's file and its passphrase typed in
(screenshots of the card before, of the confirmation and of the empty
form, never of the filled one):

- the card showed *Renewing: bundle version … goes to every node* 1.3 s
  after the click; the Controller logged `the issuing CA renewed with the
  recovery kit - "Janus fleet issuing CA 2026-10" until 2028-10-30`;
- **the six running nodes had the new bundle within the same second**,
  the oldest node (v2026.10.21) among them; each logged `access: fleet
  root …, bundle version … (2 issuing CAs) - by automation (os:admin) via
  janus-controller`, and lists both issuing CAs;
- the card then named the one node behind, `nodes_behind` said the
  same, and the switch waited: a `janusctl login` meanwhile still got a
  certificate from the CA before (correct);
- the node powered on again through the Controller: it started with its
  old bundle, and took the new one **3 min 9 s later**, by the trust
  loop; one second after, `fleet: every node has the renewal's bundle -
  janusctl's certificates now come from the issuing CA "Janus fleet
  issuing CA 2026-10"`;
- a new `janusctl login`: the certificate's issuer is the new CA, and it
  reaches all seven nodes; a context still holding a certificate of the
  CA before kept working too;
- after the switch: every node page answers, a node rebooted through its
  page (back in 8.6 s), a cluster apply (0.8 s);
- **the Controller restarted**: the new CA, the bundle version and the
  switch were all there, and every node was reached at once with the new
  CA's certificate.

No node needed the kit: the *needs the recovery kit* state and the
rescue weren't met.

## Findings

1. **The first `-advertise-address` becomes the address offered to
   nodes.** Given a name first, the Controller put it in
   `controller-info`'s `address` and would have put it in the NoCloud of
   the machines it creates (hypervisors without an address of their
   own): a name the nodes can't resolve, in the lab. The flag's help says
   so, but #451 now invites adding a name to renew the certificate
   ("no more deleting the files to add a name"). Listing the address the
   nodes use first works. Minor - a sentence in the upgrade notes or the
   README, or a separate setting for the suggested address.
2. **A node older than clusters makes its page log a 501.** The node
   page of a v2026.10.21 node (no `ClusterService`) asks
   `/api/cluster` and gets 501, logged in the console. The page works.
   Not this pre-release; an old node kept on purpose in the lab.
3. **The Fleet card's notice is taken at the answer.** Right after the
   renewal, it named five nodes as behind, though they had the bundle
   the same second; a reload showed the one node really behind. Minor.

Also: the lab's log profile still has a Logstash target outside the
lab's reach (`failing`), as in the campaigns before.

## Not tested

- **The identity's renewal at 35 days before its end**: forced through

- **BGP without BFD**: the lab's router keeps BFD on its sessions, so the
  release's hold-time wait wasn't reproduced; the `Cease` itself is
  shown by the router's log.
