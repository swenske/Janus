## Verdict

**Not ready as is.** Everything the release did, this one does as well:
the Controller's update, the image factory, the rolling update under
load (nothing lost on the VIP), the failovers, the reboots, the
throughput level with the release's. The clusters work on real nodes:
the stick table replicated and kept to the request through a rolling
apply under load, through a member destroyed and started again, and
through a member's reboot; VRRP rendered for each member; a drain moving
the VIP and the anycast route with nothing lost on the VIP; zero AVC
denials. Two findings in the clusters, though:

1. **The member serving the traffic shows its tables "catching up" for
   good** - on the Clusters page, in `janusctl cluster status` and in
   `janus_cluster_peer_table_synced` - while its peer has every update:
   janusd reads the wrong one of HAProxy's counters. Fixed in
   [#269](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/269), proven on the
   build box; `qemu-cluster-test` now checks it.
2. **A member that leaves its cluster keeps the cluster's TLS ticket
   keys**, and the members that stay go on using them for up to three
   daily rotations: a decision to take before the clusters ship
   ([below](#findings)).

A next pre-release with #269 and the ticket keys decided.

## Findings

1. **The member serving the traffic shows its tables "catching up" for
   good.** After the rolling apply, node 01 - the VIP's holder - showed
   its peer `SYNCED 0/1` in `janusctl cluster status`, **catching up**
   on the Clusters page and `janus_cluster_peer_table_synced 0`, and
   kept doing so through the rest of the campaign, while node 02 held
   every update (the same counts to the request). The node's raw
   counters for its peer: `last_pushed` and `local_update` 211,242,
   `last_acked` 0; on node 02, `last_acked` and `last_get` 211,242.
   janusd counted a peer in sync when `last_acked` reached the table's
   local updates - but in HAProxy's `show peers` (`src/peers.c`),
   `last_acked` is what this node acknowledged of the **peer's**
   updates; the peer's acknowledgement of this node's is the table's
   `update`. A member that only serves never has anything to
   acknowledge. [#269](https://github.com/swenske/Janus/pull/269) reads
   the right counter (as 32-bit counters that wrap, like HAProxy);
   proven on real HAProxy 3.4.7 output and in `qemu-cluster-test`,
   which the released janusd now fails. Nothing acts on it - the apply's
   wait reads the section's resync flags - but every cluster's busy
   member would show it, and a Grafana panel or an alert on the metric
   with it.
2. **A member that leaves keeps the cluster's TLS ticket keys.** After
   the cluster was deleted, both nodes still held the ring the cluster
   rotated on node 01 and node 02 pulled - the same SHA-256 on both.
   `ClusterLeave` deletes the membership, the member certificate and the
   cluster's CA (what its API comment promises), not
   `cluster/tls-ticket-keys`; removing a member doesn't rotate the
   others' ring either - a key stays in it for three daily rotations. So
   a node removed from a cluster - decommissioned, or reused elsewhere -
   holds, for up to three days, keys the members still issue and accept
   session tickets with: whoever gets them can read the session tickets
   those members issue (for TLS 1.2, the sessions' secrets) and make
   tickets they accept. The cluster design's sketch of the API
   ([cluster.md](../cluster.md)) has them deleted at leave. To decide: a node that leaves makes itself a fresh
   ring; removing a member replaces the others' whole ring (resumption
   lost once) or rotates it three times. Nothing released has clusters:
   decided before they ship, it needs no advisory.
3. **Each version re-applies the managed modules on every member**,
   unchanged ones too: keepalived reloaded at each apply (no state
   change - its log shows none -, only the instance's "since" reset).
   Harmless in the measures above; the files are already skipped when
   unchanged, the modules could be too.
4. **The rolling apply's wait for the tables** showed only at the
   cluster's first apply (10 s on node 01): later, each reloaded process
   had the tables from its predecessor at once - the counts exact - and
   both members took 0.4 to 0.6 s, where the notes say about 5 s each.
   The wait lasts until the tables are learnt, so nothing is wrong; the
   notes' figure is the CI tests' three-node one.
