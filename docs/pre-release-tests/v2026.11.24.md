# v2026.11.24 pre-release tests

Tested on 2026-11-24, the morning it came out, on the lab where
v2026.11.20 - the newest release - was measured first, the same
morning, with the same clients ([v2026.11.20's
report](v2026.11.20.md)). What the pre-release changes:

- **Linux 7.3.6** on the default kernel track (was 7.3.5) and
  **6.18.65** on the longterm track (was 6.18.64) - the latter with a
  🔒 for a high-rated KSM fix, in code Janus's kernel doesn't build;
- **`golang.org/x/net` 0.63.0** in janusd, janusctl, the Controller
  and the Terraform provider; the Controller's image and the
  `prometheus-node-exporter` extension built on a refreshed
  `golang:1.27.4` image;
- **lucide-react 1.77.0** in the Controller's pages (the clock icons
  redrawn).

So the kernels came first: firmware and kexec boots with their stages
on node 02 (default track) and on the Controller's longterm machine,
each node's console or kernel log checked after, on the release then on
the pre-release; HAProxy's throughput and its cost per request, since
the kernel carries every packet; the reloads under new connections and
behind a keep-alive relay, v2026.11.20's feature, as a non-regression.
Then the core campaign: the Controller's update through its updater,
its pages in both themes, the image factory, the cluster of both nodes
updated in place under load, a VRRP and a BGP failover, a rollback and
back, the nodes' health.

## Verdict

**Nothing regressed. The new kernels boot as fast as the old ones -
node 02 on 7.3.6 back in 10.0 to 10.1 s through its firmware and 7.9
to 8.8 s by kexec, the longterm machine on 6.18.65 in 5.3 to 5.4 s and
4.4 s - with zero AVC denials, no oops and CIS 33/33 on every boot;
VRRP and BGP came back after each. Throughput and the cost per request
moved within the bench's usual spread. Twenty reloads under about
12,000 new connections a second failed no request on either version,
every one handed over; behind a keep-alive relay, 0 and 0. The
Controller updated through its updater in 24.9 s (7.05 s without an
answer), runs Go 1.27.4 and `x/net` 0.63.0, and its 32 pages show no
problem in either theme; the cluster updated in place in 41.2 s with
its tables carried. The longterm 🔒 shows nowhere for a pre-release, as
designed. My opinion: ready to promote** - the decision is yours.

## Findings

None in the pre-release. Worth knowing:

1. **A single new-connection probe can go unanswered during a reload**
   with the hand-over: one of 1,394 here, one of 2,477 in v2026.11.20's
   campaign, while the node counted no drop and `wrk` saw none of its
   ~940,000 connections fail. Not this release's (same rate as the
   release), too rare to tell from the client side.
2. **The longterm 🔒 stays invisible until promotion** - by design (a
   pre-release gives no badge and no `update_available`); a reader of
   the notes looking for it on the Update page of a longterm node will
   find only the pre-release offered apart.
3. **The redrawn clock icon is behind a rare badge**: the Controller's
   only redrawn icon is the HAProxy branch's end-of-support badge,
   which no lab node shows; it was checked in the icon package, not on
   a page.

## Not tested

- A node pinned to an HAProxy branch near its end of support (to see
  the redrawn `CalendarClock` drawn): none in the lab, and none can be
  before 2028.
- KSM and swap paths of the longterm fix: not built into Janus's
  kernel.
- The longterm machine's serial console (a Controller machine's
  console isn't kept as a file on its host); its kernel log and CIS
  audit read through its page instead.
- TLS throughput: the lab's benchmark frontend is plain HTTP.
